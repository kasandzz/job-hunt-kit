# Mode: job-hunt
## Bundles
daily-core, life-admin, job-hunting, job-search (install all four in stage 5. The learning and builders bundles are left out on purpose: this notebook does one thing, finding work. The job rules are in playbook/setup/job-rules.md).
## Interview area 7 focus
The job search so far, one question at a time, in five-year-old words: where they have looked for work (newspaper, OCC, Computrabajo, Indeed, LinkedIn, friends, a job fair, the INAPAM office, a jobcentre; in Lithuania CV.lt, CVbankas.lt, CV Online or Užimtumo tarnyba); how many places they have applied to lately, and what happened; any interview coming up (company and date); whether they have their own email address and know their password (never write the password: write `(private, not saved)`); whether they have an account on OCC, Computrabajo, Indeed or LinkedIn (names of the sites only, never usernames or passwords); how sure they feel using the computer for this (all answers are fine). Never ask for ID numbers (CURP, RFC, NSS, INE, National Insurance, asmens kodas), even if a job site asks for them.
## Interview areas
These replace the base list in stage 3, step 4: same numbers, same rules. Areas 4 and 5 are about the job they want: `no opt-in` (skip step 3c for them). Ask in short, plain words. Use these as the first question or two, then follow their answers. Rules for every area: one question at a time; at most 3 options in a box, counting **tell me more** (es-MX **cuéntame más**); voice first.
1. **Your old CV:** the CV comes first, before any other question. Say: en-GB "Let's start with your CV, the paper or file that lists your jobs. Old is fine." es-MX "Empecemos con tu currículum, la hoja donde vienen tus trabajos. Aunque sea viejito, sirve." Box: en-GB "How shall we bring in your CV?" **I'll paste it or send a photo (Recommended)** / **I don't have one** / **tell me more**. es-MX "¿Cómo me pasas tu currículum?" **Lo pego o le tomo foto (Recomendado)** / **No tengo** / **cuéntame más**. "tell me more" explains the four ways, one line each:
   - Paste: open the CV, select all the words, copy and paste them here.
   - A photo of a paper CV: take a photo with the phone, send it to yourself by WhatsApp or email, save it on the computer, then drag the photo into this chat (or click the paperclip button and pick it). Read the photo carefully, word for word.
   - A file in Google Drive: if `drive: connected` in `playbook/state/connectors.md`, search it as the `tailored-cv` helper does, step 3.
   - Read it out loud: they press the Windows key and H together and read it; write what you hear.
   Save the CV text exactly as given (or as read from the photo) in `playbook/state/cv-intake.md`, under `## CV as given`, with one line `how: paste|photo|drive|spoken|none`. Then read back the jobs you found as a short list (company, job, years) and ask: en-GB "Did I read it right?" es-MX "¿Lo leí bien?" Fix what they correct; note each fix as `- fix: <what changed>` in `cv-intake.md`. "I don't have one" is fine: write `how: none` and say "No problem. We'll make one together." / "No pasa nada. Lo armamos juntos."
   Then two short questions stage 4 needs: how they like to be talked to (short and to the point / warm and chatty / tell me more) and what annoys them in a helper.
   Test mode: the CV text is in the area 1 answers of `playbook/test-answers.md` (a pasted text, or a written description of a photo). Use it as given; never ask for a file.
2. **Your working life:** go through the jobs in the CV, newest first, and fill the gaps: for each job, the year it started and ended (only what they say), what they did, and one thing they are proud of there, with a number if they have one (people, money, years, customers). Ask why a job ended only if they bring it up. Every answer line starts with the company, for example `- Ferretería López: proud of: ...`.
3. **Goals:** as base area 3. 1-3 goals for this year and 1-3 for this month; for each, why it matters and one small first step. At least one goal is about work. Stage 4 makes one note per goal.
4. **The job you want** (`no opt-in`): which jobs (2-3 kinds, in their words); where (town or area, and how far they can travel each day); full-time, part-time or by the hour; working from home is wanted, fine, or not possible; the lowest pay they would take, per month, in their money (MXN in Mexico, GBP in the UK). If they are not sure about pay, write `pay floor: not sure`. When they can start.
5. **What you won't do** (`no opt-in`): their deal-breakers, each as a short rule (for example "no night shifts", "no more than 1 hour each way", "no selling by phone"); the languages they speak and read, and how well; anything about the body they want respected at work, only if they bring it up (write it only in `interview.md`). Never ask about health.
6. **People:** as base area 6, plus one question: "Who from your old jobs would speak well of you?" (es-MX "¿Quién de tus trabajos anteriores hablaría bien de ti?"). First name, where they worked together, and whether they are still in touch. Never phone numbers or addresses. Stage 4 makes one note per person.
7. **The job search so far:** the focus above.
8. **How we search together:** what time of day suits a daily job search (stage 8 sets it; a morning time is the best guess); how many new jobs they want to see each day (at most 3 is the best guess); whether they would like you to look them up online for news, awards or articles about their work (stage 4 asks again before it searches); one hobby or thing they enjoy, for a friendly word now and then. Last, who presses the final button on a job website. Ask it once, gently: en-GB "When we apply for a job on a website, there's a last button, like Send or Apply. Before it, you always see everything and say OK twice. Who should press that last button?" es-MX "Cuando nos postulemos en una página de empleos, hay un último botón, como Enviar o Postularme. Antes de ese botón siempre ves todo y me das tu permiso dos veces. ¿Quién presiona ese último botón?" Options: **I'll always press it myself (Recommended)** / **You press it after my second OK** / **tell me more** (es-MX **Siempre lo presiono yo (Recomendado)** / **Presiónalo tú después de mi segundo permiso** / **cuéntame más**). Tell me more: en-GB "Either way nothing goes without your two OKs. On LinkedIn you always press it yourself. You can change this any time, just tell me." es-MX "De las dos formas nada sale sin tus dos permisos. En LinkedIn siempre lo presionas tú. Lo puedes cambiar cuando quieras, nada más dime." Write the answer as `submit: me` (they press it) or `submit: claude` (you press it after OK 2); not sure means `submit: me`.
